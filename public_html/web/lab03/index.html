The law of Salvage

The law of salvage dictates that a person who recovers, or helps recover, another person’s vessel or cargo in peril is entitled to a reward commensurate to the value of the property returned.

Of course, corporations have cataloged every bit of scrap in the patch, recording ownership in proprietary salvage registries. Corporations offer bounties or contracts to freelance salvage crews, who return the corporation’s property for a reward. Some crews simply search the patch for valuable scrap, paying to search the salvage registries for the rightful owner to claim their reward. Others are freebooters, salvaging from the patch whatever they want, and selling to the highest bidder—usually a rival corporation.

Ownership, Prospecting, Salvage

Ownership Rights: The owner of a wreck is the rightful proprietor of all materials, cargo, and technologies. According to the law of salvage, anything recovered from the wreck must be returned to the owner. Retaining salvage is a crime, the violating crew and vessel guilty of piracy.

Prospecting Rights: A prospector inspects a wreck, cataloging it and its contents in the salvage registry. Most prospecting rights are open, with the owner paying out a fee to prospectors for useful information.

Salvage Rights: the law of salvage requires the owner to pay a reward to the salvor. However, exclusive rights to recover salvage from a wreck can be granted by the owner. Enforcement of these rights is up to the rights-holder, with few legal protections for violators.

Clusterfuck

The patch is a nightmare to navigate. At incredible expense, a few routes have been cleared of major debris, called lines. The rest of the patch can only be navigated by experienced pilots flying small ships, mostly shuttles and nimble corvettes. Autopilot is useless, pilots have to fly stick in hand. Navigational beacons placed on landmark wrecks form a network of shuttle paths through the patch.

Gargant Cluster

Driven by equal parts desperation and entrepreneurial spirit, the first to settle the patch were freelance salvage crews known today as the Free Spacers. Led by a syndicate of captains-representative, the ‘spacers are able to negotiate reasonable prices for their salvage. For now, spacer crews are the only ones able to return salvage to corporate space.

Radburn Station

The extent of corporate dominated space. Radburn is situated at the anti-spinward edge of the Gargant Cluster. Syndicate brokerage crews haul salvage from Spinward Yards to the Corpo docks here, where scrap is sold on and salvage bounties paid out. In the corporate offices high above, the company gathers its forces to eliminate the spacers, and monopolize the material riches of the patch.

The Freeline

A toll-route carved out of the patch at great expense, monitored and patrolled by mobile judiciary and their enforcement officers. Passage can be bought only with onerous smart-scrip payments.
